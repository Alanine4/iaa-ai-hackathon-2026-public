\subsection{Yichuan's Modelling Approach}

\subsubsection{Exploratory Data Analysis}

Part 1 has 2,409,311 training rows for 2021 to 2024, 163,150 claims valued at 31 December 2024 and 626,639 test rows.
A script counts each of the ten data issues (Appendix~\ref{ov:app-dq}). A leakage scan dropped
\texttt{customer\_service\_contacts}, which averages 3.0 on claimed rows against 0.41 otherwise (C008). Bodily injury is 43\% of motor cost and only 14\% of it is reported at 30 days.

\subsubsection{Data Preparation and Feature Engineering}

All corrections live in code in \texttt{src/aiiaa/}; the raw files are never edited (C007). The module \texttt{clean}
recomputes exposure from the period dates (C001). The module \texttt{target} attaches each claim to the policy row whose
period contains the loss date, net of recoveries and without repudiated claims (C005). The module \texttt{development}
takes reported cost to ultimate (IBNR) by dividing it by the row's completeness on a cost-weighted reporting-lag curve
per line, fitted on accident year 2021 (C006). Claims are capped at the line's 99.5th percentile with a per-line load
for the excess (C014; Appendix~\ref{ov:app-p1}).

\subsubsection{Modelling Strategy}

Each line has five models (banded GLM, spline GAM, LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost), all fitted to capped cost per unit of
completeness-weighted exposure with a Tweedie loss of power 1.5 (C010). Blend weights from out-of-fold predictions are
kept only if they beat equal weights on both folds (C030); motor kept equal weights. Predictions take half the fitted trend to 2025,
and the refit averages three seeds (C019, C029). The approaches differ most in forming the prediction: Oussama's GLM splits frequency and severity and Blend4 weights four models equally, while every model here
fits pure premium directly and the weights must pass the guard.

\subsubsection{Validation}

The metric is Tweedie deviance explained ($D^2$, power 1.5). Two expanding-window folds score 2023 and 2024, with no
random split and no year feature (C017); our score \texttt{d2\_cal} rescales each fold to its actual total, so it
measures ranking. A change needed a mean gain of at least 0.002 with no fold lost (C018). S09 is the ranked entry:
private 0.13689, public 0.12863, rank 12 of 51.

\section{Part 2: Adapting to the Emerging Market}

\subsection{Task and Data}

The emerging market has 209,063 test rows and two training years, 2023 and 2024. New business is 76\% of the test rows
(15\% in Part 1) but 8\% of the training rows; motor claim frequency is 18.09\% against 13.48\%
(Appendix~\ref{ov:app-p2}).

\subsection{What Changes When the Market Changes}

The same code runs with a market setting and a shared schema (C034, C035). Each change was kept only if it passed the
adoption rule in force at the time (Section~\ref{ov:trust}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item On emerging data alone (P2-S01) the GLM and GAM overfit, with about 500 claims a year per property line.
\item Pooled training adds developed rows at weight $w$ with a market feature, re-levelled on emerging rows; the
buildings time-fold \texttt{d2\_cal} rose from 0.0410 to 0.1065 (C038, C040).
\item The property lines keep the developed half trend after the 2023 fire year; six public-board probes set motor at
its own full trend, our one board-led decision (C043).
\item Validation rows were 92\% renewals against 77\% new business in the test, so test-mix views joined the gate and
contents and motor new business got new blends (C044, C047).
\item Oussama's GAM beat the pooled CatBoost on the buildings time fold (0.1257 against 0.1176) and entered the
buildings blend (C054).
\item Averaging the tree predictions over three seeds gives P2-S15 (C057, C058).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Office Premium}

For each of the 20 reference policies, $P = (R + \mathrm{Cat} + F)/(1-k)$, where $R$ is the P2-S15 prediction and, from the emerging expense

\section{Trust, Validation and Guardrails}
\label{ov:trust}

\subsection{Validation and Adoption Rules}

Part 2 scores emerging rows on a time fold (train 2023, score 2024) and a grouped 5-fold split on
\texttt{policy\_id} (C036). Each candidate is scored on six views: the time fold and the grouped mean, each raw, re-weighted to the
2025 new-business share (about 0.77) and on new-business rows only (C044). Re-weighting tracks the board: the P2-S03 to
P2-S04 step gained 0.0154 raw, 0.0051 re-weighted and 0.0050 in public. Under rules fixed before the runs, a
change must not lose on the raw time fold, the raw grouped mean or the test-mix grouped mean, must gain 0.002 on the raw
or the test-mix grouped mean (C044), and since C053 must not lose on the time-fold test-mix and new-business views.
Gaps under 0.0005 are ties.

\subsection{Data and Code}

A SHA256 manifest, read-only attributes and a test guard every raw byte (C007); 342 pytest cases in 24 files
cover the code. The blend step rebuilds S09 byte for byte from the stored model predictions (same SHA256 as the upload; a
full refit differs slightly between runs, as the tree refits are not bit-reproducible), and P2-S14, rebuilt by two
routes, differs from the upload by at most 5.1e-10 (C058).

\subsection{Documentation and Reproduction}

\texttt{README.md} gives the task, layout, commands and known limitations. In \texttt{docs/},
\texttt{CHANGELOG.md} logs each change (C000 to C061) with reason, evidence and code location;
\texttt{AI\_LOG.md} (A01 to A37) is the AI record; \texttt{HUMAN\_REVIEW.md} lists the signed decisions R01 to R16;
\texttt{dashboard/index.html} is the evidence board; \texttt{VIDEO\_SCRIPT.md} is the video script. \texttt{reproduce.ps1} rebuilds S09 from the raw data, and with \texttt{-Part2} also P2-S13.

S09 is the ranked Part 1 entry (private 0.13689, rank 12 of 51); adding Oussama's Blend4 at 0.3 gave 0.13718 as a late

\section{Assumptions and Limitations}
\label{ov:app-lim}

Both parts assume that open-claim case estimates are unbiased and that the accident year 2021 reporting pattern holds
for later years and for the emerging market. Part 1 also assumes that the construction codes T and W both mean timber
(C004).

The emerging market's two years give one time fold, with about 500 claims per year on each property line, and 2024 is
also the least developed year. The reporting curves are borrowed, nothing beyond 24 months can be checked, and 20\% of
2024 emerging motor claims are open; the Part 2 audit puts the resulting overstatement of the 2024 ultimate at 1.8\%
for motor and 2.5\% for buildings (P2-F9). Buildings 2023 was a fire year (49 fire claims for 3.22m CU against 21 for
1.13m in 2024). The large-loss load is uniform within a line, and the emerging caps rest on 36, 6 and 6 claims above
the cap.

New-business predicted-to-actual ratios run 0.81 to 1.01 for motor, 1.30 to 2.23 for buildings and 0.74 to 0.84 for
contents, on 37 to 697 claims per line and scheme, too few to set a segment level. Part of the level rests on
public-board probes: the developed half trend (P2-S02, +0.0009, below our 0.002 rule) and the motor level. The motor
gain (+0.0029) is larger, and the model trained on 2023 had also under-predicted 2024 motor by 6\%. The risk is that a
level read from about a third of the rows does not hold on the rest; on a board of that size one line's optimal level
has a sampling standard deviation of 4.7\% for motor (audit H1). In Part 1 the private board reversed the public
preference for half trend over no trend, and the S08 probe of audit finding B4 flipped sign between the boards (public
$-0.0003$, private $+0.0001$).

Part 1 audit items left open: the trend may count the year level twice (B4; residual trend 1.017, 1.048 and 1.004
against 1.018, 1.095 and 1.064 applied). The 2025 motor large-loss load includes under-reported 2024 bodily injury and
may be 1.4\% low (B5). Recoveries appear only on closed claims, so open 2024 own-damage cost is about 1.4\% overstated
(B6). Prior-claim history is developed in cost but not in claim counts (B7). Open-claim reserving for bodily injury
cannot be verified from one snapshot, and the customer snapshot date is unknown, so early training rows may see later
information. Buildings and contents cost above the cap concentrates in the top risk decile while the load is uniform,
and the GAM uses a single penalty (C011). In the motor new-business segment, a control run made after the result was
seen scored higher on every view; choosing it would have been a post-hoc selection, so it stays a diagnostic.

\section{Part 1 Pipeline Detail}
\label{ov:app-p1}

The module \texttt{clean} recomputes \texttt{exposure} on a 365-day year, because 5,723 motor, 4,176 buildings and
4,305 contents rows held day counts or a 360-day basis (1.0139 = 365/360) (C001). It pads motor \texttt{customer\_id} to 8
characters, divides sums insured above 5 million by 1,000 and maps the construction codes T and W to timber (C002 to
C004). Repudiated claims (1,711, all with zero amounts) are excluded, and the 410 claims that match no row are listed
and left out (C005). Development lifts motor 2024 pure premium from 454 reported to 585, inside the 564 to 606 range
of 2021 to 2023 (C006), and 20.7\% of 2024 motor claims were still open at 31 December 2024. The per-line caps are 51,585,
147,445 and 28,449 CU for motor, buildings and contents, and the capped share of cost is 8.8\%, 4.2\% and 2.1\% (C014).
The feature builder joins the policy rows to the customer, address and weather tables, derives ages and adds claim
experience from earlier years only. A per-line feature policy drops columns that the trees overfitted or that carried no signal, among them
\texttt{branch\_id} on every line and motor \texttt{make\_model}; it raised mean LightGBM \texttt{d2\_cal} from 0.0832 to 0.0887 on motor, from 0.1094 to 0.1134 on
buildings and from 0.0891 to 0.0936 on contents (C026).

Each model is rescaled to its training total because log-link LightGBM ended at 0.83 of actual cost on its own training
rows; rescaling raised motor validation $D^2$ from 0.066 to 0.073 (C013). The two folds train on 2021 to 2022 and on
2021 to 2023, and each compares reported cost with the prediction times completeness (C017). Blend weights are searched
on a 0.05 grid. Motor searched weights lost to equal weights on the 2024 fold (0.09031 against 0.09071), so motor uses
0.2 for each model; buildings use GAM 0.2, LightGBM 0.25 and CatBoost 0.55, and contents GAM 0.25, LightGBM 0.2,
XGBoost 0.35 and CatBoost 0.2 (C021, C030). The S09 blends score a mean two-fold \texttt{d2\_cal} of 0.0919 for motor,
0.1144 for buildings and 0.0945 for contents. The trees early-stop on a random 10\% of the fitting rows, never on
the scored fold (C027). Half the fitted log-linear trend gives the 2025 factors 1.018 for motor, 1.095 for buildings
and 1.064 for contents (C019).

Rejected Part 1 alternatives: per-peril frequency by severity scored 0.0801 against 0.0893 on motor, 0.1045 against
0.1094 on buildings and 0.0879 against 0.0911 on contents (C023). An out-of-fold target encoding of
\texttt{make\_model} gained 0.0003 on the mean but fell on 2024 (C031). Re-tuning the trees on the final feature set
gave motor LightGBM +0.0027 on the mean but $-0.0008$ on 2024, so nothing was adopted (C032).
